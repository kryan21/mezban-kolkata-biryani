Our Story

From Lucknow's courts
to Kolkata's heart

Kolkata biryani was born in exile. When Nawab Wajid Ali Shah, the last Nawab of Awadh, came to Kolkata, his khansamas brought Lucknow's dum pukht with them — biryani sealed in a handi and slow-cooked in its own steam, fragrant rather than fiery.

In Kolkata, the biryani found its signature: the golden aloo. The potato soaked up the masalas and the meat's juices until it became, for many, the best bite in the handi — with a boiled egg keeping it company. Lighter, more delicate, unmistakably Kolkata.

At Mezban, we keep it exactly that way. Long-grain rice layered over marinated chicken and mutton, sealed and rested on dum — never heavy with spice, always fragrant to the last grain.
